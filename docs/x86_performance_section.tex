\subsection{Software Performance on x86 Processors}

We provide two implementations for the x86 platform: a portable ISO~C99
reference implementation (\texttt{ref/}) and an AVX2-optimized implementation
(\texttt{avx2\_weaver/}). The optimized build vectorizes polynomial arithmetic
and matrix sampling for $n{=}256$ (\textsc{Weaver-512} and \textsc{Weaver-1024}).
For \textsc{Weaver-2048} ($n{=}512$, $k{=}4$), it additionally provides an AVX2
NTT, SHAKE128$\times$4 parallel matrix generation, fused
\texttt{polyvec\_basemul\_acc}, and AVX2 modular reduction.
On \textsc{Weaver-512}, recent micro-optimizations add AVX2 centered-binomial
sampling for $\eta_1{=}5$, fused 9-bit public-key compression, and vectorized
message bit expansion in \texttt{poly\_frommsg}.

\subsubsection{Experimental Setup}

All measurements were taken on an AMD EPYC 7C13 (x86-64, 2.0\,GHz firmware
frequency) running Linux 6.1.0-44 (Debian~12), compiled with GCC~12.2.0
(\texttt{-O3 -march=native -fomit-frame-pointer}, AVX2/BMI2/POPCNT).
Cycle counts use \texttt{RDTSC} with overhead subtracted; each entry is the
median of $N_{\mathrm{test}}{=}1{,}000$ iterations over five consecutive runs.
Non-deterministic \texttt{randombytes} calls use Linux \texttt{getrandom(2)}
(\texttt{USE\_GETRANDOM=1}), matching the entropy backend of the ML-KEM
reference; KAT paths retain the in-tree AES-256-CTR-DRBG.
ML-KEM data are from pq-crystals/kyber commit~\texttt{4768bd3} (AVX2 build),
collected on the same machine with the same methodology.

\paragraph{Overhead decomposition.}
Table~\ref{tab:overhead_breakdown} attributes the encapsulation gap to
three categories.
%
\emph{Design-intrinsic} components---randomized lifting $\mathrm{Inv}_q$
and BCH message encoding---are absent from ML-KEM and constitute the direct
algorithmic cost of Weaver's tight MLWR reduction and aggressive compression;
together they account for $+5{,}800$ cycles at $(256,2)$ and $+13{,}100$
cycles at $(256,4)$, i.e.\ 56\,\% and 63\,\% of the total encapsulation gap.
%
\emph{Parameter-induced} overhead arises on \textsc{Weaver-512} from
$\eta_1{=}5$ (vs.\ $\eta_1{=}3$ in ML-KEM-512), adding $+2{,}060$ cycles in
CBD sampling; at $(256,4)$ both schemes use $\eta_1{=}2$, so this term
collapses to $+160$ cycles.
%
\emph{Implementation-aligned} primitives (\texttt{gen\_matrix}, NTT,
\texttt{basemul}) differ by at most 2--5\,\%, confirming that the vectorized
polynomial core is not the bottleneck.

The same split explains key generation: because $\mathrm{Inv}_q$ and BCH are
not invoked, \textsc{Weaver-1024} matches ML-KEM-1024 exactly ($1.00\times$);
the \textsc{Weaver-512} gap ($1.26\times$, $+4{,}500$ cycles) is driven by
$\eta_1{=}5$ noise expansion ($+2{,}060$ cycles) and 9-bit compressed
public-key packing ($+1{,}260$ cycles).
Note that \texttt{kem\_decaps} is faster than \texttt{kem\_encaps} on all
parameter sets because the FO re-encryption inside decapsulation omits the
\texttt{randombytes} draw present in \texttt{kem\_encaps}.

Excluding the two design-intrinsic components ($\mathrm{Inv}_q$ and BCH), the
remaining encapsulation overhead is at most $4{,}480$ and $7{,}660$ cycles on
\textsc{Weaver-512} and \textsc{Weaver-1024}, respectively ($25\%$ and $20\%$ of
the corresponding ML-KEM encapsulation medians, or $44\%$ and $37\%$ of the
measured Weaver excess).
This confirms that the performance gap is primarily a deliberate
bandwidth--security trade-off rather than a gap in core lattice arithmetic.

Component-level timings on the AVX2 build show that \texttt{gen\_matrix} is
already within $\approx 5\%$ of ML-KEM's \texttt{gen\_a} at $(256,2)$
($\approx 5.3$\,k vs.\ $\approx 5.2$\,k cycles); single-polynomial NTT and
\texttt{polyvec\_basemul\_acc} differ by at most $\approx 1.6\times$.
The remaining key-generation gap at $(256,2)$ is therefore not matrix generation
but CPA-specific work---notably $\eta_1{=}5$ noise expansion ($\approx 6.6$\,k
cycles for two polynomials vs.\ $\approx 4.6$\,k for the ML-KEM analogue) and
compressed public-key packing ($\approx 1.7$\,k cycles).

On \textsc{Weaver-2048}, end-to-end performance is dominated by matrix sampling
and serialization; \texttt{gen\_matrix} costs $\approx 47$\,k cycles per call in
the AVX2 build, and fused \texttt{polyvec\_basemul\_acc} accounts for
$\approx 6$\,k cycles per dot product at $n{=}512$.
